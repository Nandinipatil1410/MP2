TITLE: Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy and Safety of Xylara in Treating Hypertension
============================================================

Introduction:
Xylara, a novel angiotensin II receptor antagonist, was investigated in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial (NCT04567890) to assess its efficacy and safety in patients with essential hypertension. The study was conducted from February 10, 2020, to August 25, 2022.

Methods:
A total of 320 patients were enrolled, with 160 patients receiving Xylara 50mg once daily and 160 patients receiving placebo. The primary endpoint was the change in seated systolic blood pressure (SSBP) from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints included the change in seated diastolic blood pressure (SDBP) and the incidence of adverse events.

Results:
Demographics: The mean age of the patients was 58.2 years (SD: 10.5), with 55.6% males and 44.4% females. The mean baseline SSBP was 152.1 mmHg (SD: 12.8) and the mean baseline SDBP was 95.6 mmHg (SD: 8.5). The study population was predominantly Caucasian (85.6%), with 10.3% African American, and 4.1% Asian.

Efficacy: At week 12, the mean change in SSBP was -15.4 mmHg (SD: 10.9) in the Xylara group and -5.1 mmHg (SD: 9.5) in the placebo group (p < 0.001). The mean change in SDBP was -9.2 mmHg (SD: 7.3) in the Xylara group and -3.5 mmHg (SD: 6.8) in the placebo group (p < 0.01). A total of 120 patients (75%) in the Xylara group achieved a SSBP < 140 mmHg, compared to 60 patients (37.5%) in the placebo group.

Safety: The incidence of adverse events was similar between the Xylara group (45.6%) and the placebo group (42.5%). The most common adverse events were headache (12.5% vs 10.6%), dizziness (8.1% vs 6.3%), and fatigue (6.3% vs 5.6%).

Discussion:
Xylara demonstrated significant reductions in SSBP and SDBP compared to placebo. The safety profile of Xylara was similar to that of placebo. However, a subtle nuance was observed, where patients with a baseline SSBP ≥ 160 mmHg (n = 80) had a greater response to Xylara, with a mean change in SSBP of -20.5 mmHg (SD: 12.1), compared to patients with a baseline SSBP < 160 mmHg (n = 80), who had a mean change in SSBP of -10.3 mmHg (SD: 9.5).

Conclusion:
Xylara is an effective and safe treatment for patients with essential hypertension. Further studies are needed to fully elucidate the potential benefits of Xylara in specific patient populations.
